A note from the curator

Listen closely. Look carefully.

The Sound Cabinet brings my phonetics projects and useful external resources together in one place. Its name borrows the spirit of the cabinet of curiosities: a place to collect, compare, and ask questions.

External entries are selected from their published descriptions. They have not yet undergone a systematic hands-on evaluation. Each entry links to its original source; access and features may change.

Field notes

How to use the cabinet.

  1. 01
    Hear the reference.Start with an IPA recording or a language-specific example. Notice the contrast you want to practise.
  2. 02
    Observe one feature at a time.Use vowel formants, consonant timing, or a pitch contour to investigate a specific question.
  3. 03
    Treat the plot as evidence.A curve is a measurement, not a verdict. Recording conditions, context, and tracking errors affect what you see.
