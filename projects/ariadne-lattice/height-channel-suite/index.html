Observed issues

  • All three restricted seed-42 runs crossed their height gates, but required more decisions than their corresponding controls: map24 74→83, map32 79→96, map6 66→77. These single pairs do not establish average performance.
  • Map32 changed target height 37 times, versus 22 in its control. Some late alternations made little horizontal progress, but observations still changed; they cannot all be classified as wasted.
  • One map32 rotor contact tick occurred at 4.04 s, far from the new gates. Decision 4 selected local waypoint (14,9,1) inside an original wall; an earlier observed map marked that voxel free, and a later map marked it occupied. The exact contacted primitive was not recorded.
  • The extra map24 seed-43 run crashed in the native planning process during the first post-motion frontier update. It is an execution failure, not evidence that the policy failed to navigate. Exact raw inputs were not saved, so the cause remains unresolved; later tests add full planner-input recording.

How to interpret these runs

All actions are generated by the trained policy. Each variant is limited to 128 waypoint decisions. Map 24 includes one additional restricted run with seed 43 to expose possible variation. A pair of runs is not enough to claim a statistically reliable performance difference.

Triggering the planner stop condition is not proof of complete exploration. Observed free volume is not a validated coverage percentage. Rotor sensors do not provide a complete collision guarantee. Frequent height switching may gather useful observations or may be inefficient; height traces alone cannot distinguish the two.
