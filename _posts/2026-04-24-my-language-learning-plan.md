This post is a "Language Learning Scheme" I plan for myself at university.

Thanks to the vast number of international and exchange students at NUS, practicing languages with native speakers becomes way easier than before, which also reignites my dormant hobby of learning new languages.

Prior to university, I was only fluent in two languages, Mandarin Chinese and English. They are either my mother tongue or the language I started to be exposed to at a really young age. I did pick up some Japanese and Spanish, mostly unofficially, during my secondary school years, whereas I was just at a very layman's level.

Around a month ago, an urge to learn a second foreign language systematically bumped into my head. I decided to continue on Spanish, since it's one of the languages that I had quite a little exposure to, both phonetically and grammatically. By utilizing AI tools like ChatGPT and online learning websites, I can now write short essays in Spanish using different tenses except subjunctivo(I guess I just reached CEFR A2 level in terms of lexicon). Thus, I set a small goal for myself: to reach B2 level before finishing my undergraduate studies. Also, I am picking up (sometimes actively learning) Japanese and Cantonese (if you consider it as a distinct language instead of a dialect) for daily conversation with my Japanese and Malaysian friends. So I am also anticipating my Japanese to reach around N2 level 3 years later :)

I am adopting the grammar-first language learning methodology, since it's more efficient for an engineering student like me. I enjoy the similarities and discrepancies between different languages and also etymology- I have to admit that my language learning is somewhat linguistics-oriented. That's true to some extent, and I do find great ecstasy in learning those seemingly miscellaneous things!
