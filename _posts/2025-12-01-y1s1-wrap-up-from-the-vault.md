Freshing off the tireness of the whole semester, I went to swim in the USC swimming pool tonight, not leaving till their closing time. The everlasting one-north summer always warms up the water temperature, making the pool my heaven to work out and meditate in. Every glittering, unresting wave, bouncing back after hitting the wall, reminded me of the agony of my DE exam, and ridiculously, the epitaph “A man whose name was writ in water” by Keats.

It has been four months sharp since I kicked off my university life with a relentless wish to achieve something and to discover the specific field that really intrigues me. So far so good.

Before coming, I chose to live on campus in Raffles Hall based on its proximity to libraries, lecture theatres, and sports hall, which proved to be a bittersweet choice, that’s to say, harsh living condition but great people.

My room temperature is 31C the time I am writing down these words, which feels even worse as humidity soars in rainy seasons. Also, I’ve been friends with spiders, squirrels, and even snakes in these days. The assault of little bugs and scorching heat waves make me tossing and turning all night long–sometimes lead to insomnia.

Notwithstanding the hell-like living conditions, there’re so many brilliant people and jack-of-all-trades around me every day! As who has taken part in some physics Olympics, I’m always having a special feeling of admiration for those IPho participants. The good thing is I have met a lot of them here– both full-time students and exchangers, some of whom have also offered me adoptable advice of learning physics and pursuing a Ph.D. Also there are students from China, some coming here right after high school, some coming after spending 1 or 2 years uni life in Chinese unis, such as SJTU and FDU, who are really hardworking though already being so smart!

We need to dine together every morning and evening due to our compulsory meal plan. We also share a commual study room with full AC on(which is a life saver for me in those nights when I’m losing sleep). So, I had some really good time studying, living, and playing with my friends–and I really miss some of my exchanger friends since they’ll leave nus in next semester.

Throughout the learning journey of this semester, I found that engineering is somewhat boring and not up to my taste. After being exposed to more theoretical physics knowledge, I felt there’s a world apart between physics and electrical engineering, in terms of methodology and aesthetics. Diving more deeper and learning more knowledge, I also found that researches done by CQT at NUS on quantum computing is really intriguing! Thus, I consulted with my academic advisor, which appeared to be a really nice meeting, got some advice on major choice and formed a self study plan. Moreover, the activity I am preparing for now is also related to that field!

There is a plethora of things that I want to say in this post. However, retrospecting on past is not a good practice for the young…… so I’ll just stopped here and save my words for the years to come 🙂

The most tragedic thing for my vacation is it’s only 1 month! Again, let me chime in, I really miss the food in Shenyang!
